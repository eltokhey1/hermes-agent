## II. Character identity consistency — five graduated prompts

**1 — lighting only**

~~~text
Use the authorized reference portrait as identity anchor.
Keep exact recognizable face, age, proportions, expression, hairstyle
and texture. Change only illumination to gentle studio lighting.
Do not beautify or alter facial features.
~~~

**2 — background only**

~~~text
Keep the reference person visually unchanged. Replace only the background
with a modern office. Match realistic depth, perspective, shadows and
original body outline. Preserve hair, clothing, facial marks and identity.
~~~

**3 — clothing**

~~~text
Change clothing to a navy business suit and white shirt on the
authorized reference person. Preserve facial landmarks, hairstyle,
body proportions, age and natural skin tone. Maintain true-to-photo
perspective; no face regeneration or identity substitution.
~~~

**4 — scene and camera angle**

~~~text
Create a realistic three-quarter portrait at a trade conference
of the same reference person. Preserve distinctive facial proportions,
eye spacing, jawline, nose, lips, age, complexion and hairline.
If the angle is underconstrained, prefer identity conservation
over inventing new facial features.
~~~

**5 — four-frame batch consistency**

~~~text
Create 4 coordinated portraits of the authorized reference person:
office, logistics terminal, conference, and neutral studio.
Identity-lock appearance: facial geometry, complexion, hairstyle,
distinctive marks, approximate age. Vary only environment, lighting,
appropriate clothing and camera composition. Review all four for drift.
No new person, synthetic endorsement, extra fingers, text or watermark.
~~~
